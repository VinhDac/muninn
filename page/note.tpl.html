I · Huginn's best flight

The winner looked like a discovery.

Huginn is the search. It flew seven families of macro signals over fifteen ETFs in equities, bonds, commodities and currencies: trend, moving-average crosses, reversals, cross-sectional momentum, trend switched by a regime, one market leading another, and a macro indicator timing a market. Each family came in several look-backs, asset groups, volatility settings and rebalancing speeds: {{FLIGHTS_SEEN}} flights, {{FLIGHTS}} of them in the market often enough to judge.

Muninn is the ledger. It keeps every flight and flies it again on {{ROTATIONS}} calendars rotated by a year or more. There a signal keeps its habits, its costs and its likeness to every other flight, but it cannot know the next day. The best flight a whole search finds on such a calendar is the bar.

Fly a night where nothing can be predicted

Each night is one rotated calendar. The whole search flies again, and the best flight lands on the line. The amber mark is the real winner.

III · What survives is the flock

Judge the family, not the bird.

A flight's rotated calendars keep everything about it except timing, so its Sharpe minus theirs is the part only timing explains. Averaged over a family, that margin held. IV · Muninn in the agent loop

Not a formula. A habit.

The agent writes down every flight, and the ledger flies them again where nothing can be predicted.

from muninn import Ledger

book = Ledger(returns, split="2016-12-31")      # {{ROTATIONS}} rotated calendars
for idea in agent.propose():                    # Huginn: every idea, kept or not
    book.record(idea.name, idea.family, idea.weights)

book.p_value({{WIN_IN}})    # {{P_DEC}}: luck finds a better best on {{P_PCT}} of calendars
book.bar(0.95)        # {{BAR95}}: the best flight luck finds, 95th percentile
book.families()       # each family's Sharpe minus its own rotated calendars
  1. Record every flight, the failures too. The bar is set by all of them, and an agent that adapts to what it has seen hides most of its flights unless something writes them down.
  2. Measure the bar, don't assume it. Counted three ways, the same {{FLIGHTS}} flights are {{PR_TRIES}}, {{LJ_TRIES}} or {{ROT_TRIES}} independent tries, and the bar moves from {{PR_BAR}} to {{ROT_BAR}}. The rotated calendars need no count: they already know how alike the flights are. Here they took {{LEDGER_SECONDS}} seconds on a laptop.
  3. Promote families, not winners. A family's margin over its own rotated calendars is the part of its Sharpe that is timing.
  4. Track forward what was promoted, against the bar of the search that found it.
